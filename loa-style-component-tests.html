Somewhere between New England cedar shingles and the loose fit of a favorite pair of jeans, Loose Fit speaks to the familiarity and nostalgia of our collective favorite things. It borrows from the language of domestic siding and soft clothing alike, landing between architecture and garment, enclosure and embrace. The project asks what happens when a typically taut, weatherproof surface is a little softer, when a façade becomes pliable, padded, and permissive.

Eighty puffy shingles are generously stuffed with recycled denim, repurposed from fabric waste from the garment industry. Celebrating these afterlives, the pavilion borrows techniques from garment tailoring and sail fabrication, learning from stitching patterns that shape volume while anticipating wind and stretch. Lavender nylon tie-back loops, engineered and sewn in like sailboat reinforcements, secure the shingles during high wind events. A layer of shredded, recycled foam wraps the exterior in subtle chromatic speckling, revealing a collage alongside cobalt blue marine canvas and glossy transparent marine fabric.

Casually draped over a fabric-tailored frame, each shingle remains free for interaction. The pavilion invites proximity rather than distance: to walk alongside it, to bump into it, to lean, hug, and lounge between its pillow-like walls. Inside, the dense layers of insulation unexpectedly dampen the city beyond, transforming an urban site into a soft, quiet interior.
